NASA Flight Safety Directive: Key Paradigms for Moon, Mars & Deep Space

Source: NASA Glenn Research Center Combustion Branch
01 / The Optical Detector Blindspot

Standard Earth smoke detectors rely on thermal buoyancy to carry soot upward into ionization or light-scattering chambers. In 0g, soot aggregates locally and does not rise. Flames can remain undetected inside equipment racks until forced ventilation activates, triggering catastrophic flashovers.

02 / The Extinguisher Paradox

Directly discharging high-velocity CO₂ or water mists in microgravity can inject turbulent kinetic energy into a quiescent flame, introducing fresh oxygen and scattering smoldering debris across the cabin. AI-guided slow-purge nitrogen flooding is mandatory to safely quench flames without dispersal.

03 / Partial Gravity Hazard on Moon & Mars

Moon (0.16g) and Mars (0.38g) represent the most dangerous flammability envelope. They have just enough buoyant draft to feed fresh oxygen to the flame, but not enough convection to cool the fuel surface through turbulent heat transfer, resulting in higher flame spread rates than Earth (1g) in low-speed winds.
